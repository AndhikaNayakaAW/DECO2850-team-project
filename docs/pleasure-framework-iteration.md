### Not this
Experiences the data warns us away from. Each would turn care into control.

- **No verdicts.** Being told what to answer. Participants own their reasons and resent being managed twice.
- **No manager view.** A dashboard of my energy that my workplace can see is surveillance in a care costume.
- **No scores.** Streaks, points or a wellness percentage. Tiredness is not a failure to be gamified.
- **No forced horizon.** A month-long plan imposed on people who live week to week and find that exhausting (P5).

## Step 2 · Persona: Dinda Pratiwi

A composite of the five interviewees. Closest to P1 (jewellery retail, one year), with the money check from P5, the manager guilt from P2, the travel problem from P3 and the rehearsed refusals from P4.

> "I prioritise my health. But weekend shifts pay better, and I felt bad saying no."

| | |
|---|---|
| Age, pronouns | 21, she/her |
| Study | Third-year undergraduate, international student |
| Lives | Share house with two roommates, 35 minutes by bus from campus |
| Work | Casual retail (jewellery, Queen Street), one year. Occasional café shifts. |
| Roster | Employer app, one to two weeks ahead. Same-day fill-ins by text from the manager. |
| Essentials | $760 a fortnight: rent $520, groceries and bus $240 |

**What she wants**
- Cover essentials every fortnight without thinking about it daily (P5)
- Keep Saturday shifts; weekend rates make the week worth it (P1)
- Keep Sunday as rest, not a spare day for fill-ins (P4)
- Get her Pilates membership back once hours are steady (P1)
- Pass her units without cramming around closes (P2, P5)
- Stay in her manager's good books without living at the store (P2)

**What wears her down**
- The dash from tutorial to shift, sometimes running for the bus (P1, P3)
- Same-day asks when she is already spent (P1, P4)
- Hours falling from 24 a week to 3 after Christmas (P1)
- Rehearsing a decline message in her head before sending it (P4)
- Guilt after saying no to a manager she likes, or saying yes and regretting it (P2)
- Standing five hours on a quiet floor, then blanking out with customers (P1)

**What she already does**
- Blocks shifts in Google Calendar with travel time either side (P1, P5)
- Reads weekend shifts and total hours first (P1)
- Avoids being rostered straight after tutorials (P1)
- Asks herself "have I earned enough for rent?" before accepting (P5)
- Buys her favourite meal and sleeps early after a bad close (P1)
- Talks about tiredness with colleagues her own age (P1, P2)

**In her words (verbatim from transcripts)**
- "I have to prioritise my health because working in retail can be very exhausting." (P1)
- "Have I earned enough money for my rent and my living expenses?" (P5)
- "I felt bad saying no to this manager. She is really nice to me. So I stayed. I extended my shift." (P2)
- "I try to come up with the best way to reply before I cancel the shift." (P4)
- "Usually it's on me. I never really blame the roster, because they roster me based on my own availability." (P4)

**Who cares for whom**
- Dinda cares for: her manager and team (covers, stays late), her uni group (moves a Saturday recording), her roommates, herself (rest, food, sleep, gym).
- Who cares for Dinda: colleagues who share the tiredness, friends who reschedule, group members who "thankfully understood". Not on the list: the roster app.
- Where care goes missing: the minutes between a shift offer and her reply. She weighs body, uni, rent and her manager's feelings alone, then writes the message alone. That is the moment the prototype is for.

## Step 3 · Four Pleasures (Jordan, 2000), applied

| Pleasure | What the data says | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| **Physio** (body, senses) | Standing 5 h on a quiet floor (P1); running for the bus (P1); close at 6 pm, open at 6 am (P2); favourite meal and early night (P1); late shifts and the trip home (P4) | Travel buffers, closes flagged as closes, recovery ritual after long shifts |
| **Socio** (relationships) | Colleagues the same age and country (P1); a favourite manager you can't say no to (P2); group members who understood (P1); a visiting friend (P2); no friends at work as a foreigner (P5) | Offer another time instead of a flat no; half-written messages to friends and groups |
| **Psycho** (thoughts, feelings) **← applied** | Deciding before replying: rent, health, mood (P1, P5); rehearsing the decline (P4); guilt after no, relief after yes (P2); blaming yourself, never the roster (P3, P4); four weeks ahead is "too far, exhausting" (P5) | Show the trade-off without making the call; write the message; make rest and money legible in words |
| **Ideo** (values, identity) | "I prioritise my health" (P1); "I prioritise my uni" (P4); "It's a job, I signed up for that" (P2); honesty vs "I'm sick" (P5); care is not surveillance (brief) | Monthly priorities set the defaults; honest scripts; privacy stance on the screen |

### Why psycho-pleasure
1. **It is where the hardest moment sits.** Every participant described the gap between an offer and a reply as something they carry alone. It is cognitive and emotional, not physical or social.
2. **It is what the roster apps ignore.** Workforce, MyJob and manager texts show slots and pay. None show how the worker is doing, so the worker has nowhere to put that information.
3. **It can be received as care without control.** Helping someone feel at ease with a decision needs no reporting to anyone. Physio and socio features drift toward tracking and group feeds; this one doesn't have to.

### Seven design moves

| Principle | In the prototype | Screen | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Show the trade-off, don't make the call | Body, Week and Money as three sentences. No score, no recommendation. Three answers of equal weight. | Shift offer | P1, P5, P3 |
| Take the words out of the worry | A reply composer that writes the honest message, Warm or Plain, editable, sent from her own app. | Reply | P4, P5, P2 |
| Rest in words, not a score | "Tuesday's close is still in your legs", never "Energy 62%". | Week, Offer | P1, brief |
| Rest is booked, not leftover | A protected day sits in the week like a shift; the app asks before she gives it away. | Week, Money & rest | P4, P3 |
| Money as "covered by", not a target | Essentials for the fortnight, earned and booked, plus a heads-up before the January dip. | Money & rest | P1, P5 |
| Ask after, not during | Three taps after a shift. The recovery kit comes from her own answers. | Check-in | P1 |
| Yours, not theirs | No manager view. The only things that leave the app are messages she sends herself. | Me, every screen | Brief |

## Step 4 · Prototype update: Offshift

**Concept.** Offshift sits beside the employer's roster app. It never talks to the manager. It cares for the one moment every participant described carrying alone: a shift offer arrives, and you have to decide and reply.

**The week in 3D.** Above the phone, a draggable three-dimensional model of Dinda's week (6 am to midnight, to scale) shows uni, shifts, tonight's offer and the protected Sunday as blocks. Hovering a block shows its detail; tapping the amber offer opens the Shift offer screen. It falls back to a flat chart where WebGL is unavailable and stays still under reduced-motion settings.

**Screens**
1. **Week.** Opens on how she is (Body, Money as two plain-words tiles), the manager's ask as a card she opens when ready, and the week with Sunday written in as rest.
2. **Shift offer.** Three checks in one sentence each: Body (Tuesday's close still in her legs), Week (tute ends 3:00, bus 35 min, arrives 3:50 with no time to eat), Money (essentials covered by Saturday; tonight adds about $160). Three equal buttons: Not tonight / Offer another time / Take it.
3. **Reply.** The composer writes the honest message in a Warm or Plain tone. She edits, copies, and sends from her own messaging app. Offshift never sends on her behalf.
4. **Check-in.** After a shift: Fine / Tired / Wiped, one thing for tonight from her recovery kit, done. Feeds the Body check next time.
5. **Money & rest.** Essentials bar for the fortnight (earned, booked, "covered by Saturday"), a heads-up for the January dip, the protected Sunday and the exam block.
6. **Me.** Health first / Uni first / Money first sets which check leads. Recovery kit. "Who sees this: only you."

**What the lens changed**
- Removed: any "you should decline" text; a numeric energy or wellness score; a manager-side or team-side dashboard; streaks and "rest days kept" counters.
- Reframed: a decline button became a reply composer with three honest options; a budget tracker became one line ("covered by Saturday"); mood tracking became a three-tap check-in that only feeds the next offer.
- Added: travel time inside the Week check; a protected day the app asks before breaking; a recovery kit built from what helped before; monthly priorities that set the defaults.

**Next: test it against the pleasure it claims**

| Task | Watch for |
|---|---|
| "Jess asks you to cover tonight. Decide and reply." | Do they read all three checks? Do they edit the message or send it as written? Did they want the app's opinion? |
| "You've just finished a close. Do the check-in." | Is "Wiped" comfortable to admit? Does the recovery kit feel like theirs or like advice? |
| "Set what matters this month, then find out who can see it." | Do they trust the privacy line? Would they turn the January heads-up off? |

| Criterion | Measure | Why it matters for care |
|---|---|---|
| "It felt like my call." | 1–5 after Task 1 | Care that decides for you is control. |
| "I'd send this message as is." | Yes / with edits / no | The words have to be theirs, not the app's. |
| "Anything felt like being watched?" | Open answer after Task 3 | The brief's line between care and surveillance. |
| Time from offer to reply | Seconds, against their usual | Psycho-pleasure shows up as less rehearsal. |
