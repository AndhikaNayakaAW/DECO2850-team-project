Screen 1 · Week

Open on how she is, not how much she'll earn.

Design move
Body and Money are two tiles in plain words. The manager's ask is a card she opens when ready, not a notification that demands a reply. Sunday is written into the week as rest, so it needs no defending.
Pleasure
psycho The week reads as hers. Secondary physio: closes are named as closes, because they cost the body more than the hours suggest.
Evidence
The weekend shifts and number of hours are the two things that I always look at first.P1
It is more because I am tired and I just want to, like, lie down.P1
I try to avoid working on Sundays, just because I need to rest.P4
Care check
Who is caring for whom: Dinda for herself. The roster app shows slots and pay. This shows the person in the slots.
Screen 2 · Shift offer

Show the trade-off. Don't make the call.

Design move
Three checks, Body, Week and Money, each one sentence. No score, no “we recommend”. Three honest answers with equal weight, and “take it” is a real option, not a guilt trip.
Pleasure
psycho The decision is made with the facts in view, so it feels like a choice rather than a reflex. That is the pleasure the current roster apps skip entirely.
Evidence
I was thinking, “Have I earned enough money for my rent and my living expenses?”P5
The first thing that went through my mind was of course my health.P1
It takes around one and a half hours to travel from my university to my workplace.P3
I felt bad saying no to this manager… so I stayed. I extended my shift.P2
Care check
Care for herself and care for Jess are both on the screen. Neither is shamed. The app has no stake in the answer.
Screen 3 · Reply

Take the words out of the worry.

Design move
The composer writes the message she was going to rehearse anyway: honest, short, kind. Warm or Plain. She edits it, copies it, and sends it from her own messaging app.
Pleasure
psycho Relief. ideo Honesty instead of “I'm sick”. socio Offering another time keeps her a good colleague.
Evidence
I try to think about what to say to my manager before I tell them, and I try to come up with the best way to reply.P4
Then I will just say I'm sick and change to another time.P5
I offered to do a shift swap… I know the managers would be understanding.P2
Care check
Offshift never messages the manager itself. The words leave only when Dinda sends them, so she keeps her voice and her relationship.
Screen 4 · Check-in

Ask after, not during. Three taps.

Design move
Body state and one thing for tonight, then done. The recovery kit is built from her own past answers, offered and never pushed. What she records feeds the Body check next time Jess asks.
Pleasure
physio The meal and the early night. Delivered through psycho: someone noticed that five hours standing on a quiet floor is work.
Evidence
I bought my favourite meal, went home and slept early. Eating food that I like and getting enough sleep really helps me stay in a good mood.P1
The store was pretty quiet, so I had to stand there for a long time.P1
I ended a shift at 6 PM last night and then I'm starting 6 AM the next day.P2
Care check
Care, not cure. No graphs, no streaks, no “wellness score”. Tiredness is accepted as part of the job and made visible to her only.
Screen 5 · Money & rest

“Covered by”, not a target to chase.

Design move
Income is shown against essentials for the fortnight, the unit casual pay actually arrives in. The post-Christmas dip becomes a heads-up months early. Rest days and exam blocks sit beside money because they compete for the same hours.
Pleasure
psycho Calm. Once essentials are covered, the extra shift becomes optional, and the Body check can be heard.
Evidence
After Christmas, I sometimes only received five hours, three hours or six hours in one week… I had to cancel [Pilates].P1
If I'm all good and I already have plans with my friends… I will reject the shift.P5
Especially if I have exams coming up. It's good to plan it out.P2
Care check
Shows enough to decide, never a budget lecture. What counts as essential is hers to set.
Screen 6 · Me

Her values set the defaults. Not the app's.

Design move
“Health first”, “Uni first” or “Money first” changes which check leads and what gets flagged. The privacy line is on the screen, not buried in a policy.
Pleasure
ideo Participants described their priorities as identity. psycho Trust that recording a bad night has no consequence at work.
Evidence
I have to prioritise my health because working in retail can be very exhausting.P1
I try to prioritize my uni. So if there are two things at the same time, I usually just prioritize uni.P4
Many “caring” technologies slide easily into surveillance if they prioritise the carer's peace of mind over the autonomy of the person being cared for.Brief
Care check
The cared-for person has a voice and can push back: she can change the priorities, edit every message, and turn every nudge off.

Not this

Experiences the data warns us away from. Each one would turn care into control.

  • No verdictsBeing told what to answer. Participants own their reasons and resent being managed twice.
  • No manager viewA dashboard of my energy that my workplace can see. That is surveillance wearing a care costume.
  • No scoresStreaks, points or a wellness percentage. Tiredness is not a failure to be gamified.
  • No forced horizonA month-long plan imposed on people who live week to week and find that exhausting.

Step 3 · Jordan's Four Pleasures, applied

Where the pleasure is missing.

Patrick Jordan's framework asks what kind of pleasure a product could give beyond being usable. We mapped all five transcripts against the four, then chose the one with the most unmet moments. It is not the obvious one for a rest-and-wellbeing project.

physio Physio-pleasure

Body and senses: touch, taste, comfort, fatigue.

  • Standing five hours on a quiet floor P1
  • Running for the bus after a tute P1
  • Close at 6 pm, open at 6 am P2
  • The favourite meal and the early night P1
  • Late shifts and the trip home P4
OpportunityTravel buffers, closes flagged as closes, a recovery ritual after long shifts.

socio Socio-pleasure

Relationships, belonging, being a good colleague.

  • Colleagues the same age, same country P1
  • A favourite manager you can't say no to P2

  • Guilt after saying no, relief after saying yes P2

Why psycho-pleasure

It is where the hardest moment sits.Every participant described the gap between an offer and a reply as the moment they carry alone. It is cognitive and emotional, not physical or social.
It is what the roster apps ignore.Workforce, MyJob and manager texts show slots and pay. None of them show how the worker is doing, so the worker has nowhere to put that information.
It can be received as care without control.Helping someone feel at ease with a decision needs no reporting to anyone. Physio and socio features drift toward tracking and group feeds. This one doesn't have to.

What the lens changed

Removed
  • Any “you should decline” text
  • A numeric energy or wellness score
  • A manager-side or team-side dashboard
  • Streaks and “rest days kept” counters
Reframed
  • A decline button became a reply composer with three honest options
  • A budget tracker became one line: covered by Saturday
  • Mood tracking became a three-tap check-in that only feeds the next offer
Added
  • Travel time inside the Week check
  • A protected day the app asks before breaking
  • A recovery kit built from what helped before
  • Monthly priorities that set the defaults
